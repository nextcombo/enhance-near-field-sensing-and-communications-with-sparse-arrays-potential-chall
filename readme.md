# Enhance Near-Field Sensing and Communications with Sparse Arrays: Potential, Challenges, and Emerging Trends [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: It is anticipated that the sixth-generation (6G) communication will support a variety of applications, such as ultra-high definition video streaming, augmented reality, autonomous vehicles, and Internet of Things connectivity. To ensure stable and reliable high data rate services, there is an urgent need to achieve breakthroughs in large bandwidth millimeter wave (mmWave)/terahertz (THz) technology. Additionally, 6G introduces network sensing, expanding beyond communication and achieving applications such as positioning, imaging, environmental monitoring, and gesture/activity recognition. The evolution towards 6G offers tremendous potential for various applications, with technological advancements unlocking transformative experiences and functionalities.
Approximately 15 years ago, researchers began to delve deeply into the spherical wave (SW) propagation domain of millimeter wave line-of-sight (LoS) communication [1]. These investigations showcased impressive multi-carrier capability inherent in SW channels, presenting a promising opportunity to enhance high-frequency communication systems. The unique characteristics of SW propagation, such as the ability to simultaneously support multiple data streams, have sparked interest in exploring its communication technology potential. Overall, these pioneering findings pave the way for further exploitation of SW propagation to improve the capabilities and efficiency of high-frequency communication systems, thereby setting the stage for the future development of this field.
Recently, there has been significant attention on super-large (XL) arrays, with a focus on the propagation of SW plane waves in the near field region. This region is defined by the Fraunhofer distance (also known as the Rayleigh distance), which represents the minimum distance required to maintain the phase difference between the received signals across array elements at maximum π/8. The large aperture of XL arrays leads to an amplification of the Fraunhofer distance that needs to be confirmed within typical communication coverage ranges, as its impact cannot be ignored. Moreover, XL arrays with a large number of antennas offer advantages such as improved signal quality, reduced interference, and enhanced spatial multiplexing capabilities. They not only enhance communication systems but also hold great promise for sensor applications [2] [3]. One important aspect is range estimation. By leveraging the near-field effect, narrowband distance estimation becomes feasible, enabling applications such as object positioning, target tracking, and proximity sensing. The combination of SW propagation and XL arrays opens up new possibilities for high-frequency communication and advanced sensor technologies.
The use of dense uniform arrays (DUAs) with half-wavelength spacing faces two main limitations: high hardware costs and energy consumption due to the multitude of antennas, and limited medium to long-distance communication using SW properties, along with limited Fraunhofer boundaries. Overcoming these challenges is crucial for advancing XLarrays. Solutions include exploring alternative array configurations, array synthesis techniques, and innovative approaches beyond traditional DUA. This will unlock the potential of SW propagation and support high-frequency S&C applications. Sparse arrays (SA), as counterparts to DUAs, perform well in array signal processing tasks due to their higher degrees of freedom (DoF) and resolution. However, their full potential in communication systems, especially in XL arrays, remains unexplored.
This paper outlines the hardware/algorithm challenges and fundamental potential in near-field S&C. It also explores the prospects of using SA for XL arrays (referred to as XL-SA) to unearth their latent potential and inspire further research and innovation in this field. Specifically, we delve into the SA of XL systems, including: 1) an introduction to classical SA, 2) a comparison between XL-SA and XLDUAs on some key features, 3) the integration of SA in XL systems, and 4) the potential and application prospects of XL-SA. To support our findings, we conducted two simulations focusing on channel rank and CRB to demonstrate the potential of XL-SA in near-field S&C. Ultimately, we identified key challenges and corresponding potential research directions for enhancing XL-SA in near-field S&C.
Near-field XL Array: A blessing in disguise
The original intention of the XL array was to leverage the compact size advantage of high-frequency antennas while increasing their number to achieve significant beamforming gains. This innovative approach aimed to address the inherent challenges associated with high losses in high-frequency communication.
However, a large number of antennas pose significant hardware system challenges, including cost, complexity, and design. Moreover, compared to traditional arrays, the extensive array aperture introduces deviations in electromagnetic propagation, thereby amplifying the complexity of signal processing. Nevertheless, challenges often come with opportunities. By leveraging near-field effects, XL arrays unlock other benefits beyond their substantial beamforming gains.
A. Hardware Constraints
Despite employing low-complexity hybrid beamforming, in millimeter-wave/terahertz systems, XL MIMO faces more hardware design challenges than massive MIMO.
1) Hardware Cost: In the hybrid beamforming architecture, each antenna requires important components: at least one power amplifier/low noise amplifier for signal transmission or reception and a phase shifter for direction control. As the number of antennas increases, the demand for phase shifters and power amplifiers/low noise amplifiers linearly increases, leading to significant hardware costs and higher energy consumption.
2) Control complexity: Consideration of phase errors caused by manufacturing tolerances and material defects is necessary. These random errors have a multiplicative impact on the beam pattern, necessitating algorithmic compensation [4]. However, managing multiple phase shifters in an XL array simultaneously presents a more challenging task. Additionally, actual power amplifiers introduce nonlinearity, leading to signal distortion and interference with adjacent tones. As an effective solution, digital pre-distortion mitigates this situation by preprocessing signals based on captured power amplifier characteristics. However, the downside of digital pre-distortion is its increased computational complexity for antennas, and the use of ultra-wide bandwidth may further limit its application in XL arrays. Array diagnostics are another challenge, involving blocking particle detection, identifying power loss or phase shift due to blocking particles. In the near field, fast and reliable algorithms pose challenges for continuous monitoring, identification of anomalies, and ensuring the efficient operation of the XL system.
3) Physical design: In practice, there are certain manufacturing challenges. Integrating multiple antenna elements within an array leads to significant upgrades in the complexity of routing circuit components and antenna elements. Moreover, the increase in the number of components concentrated in limited space also raises cooling requirements for these loops. Therefore, the design and implementation of such an XL array require careful attention to ensure efficient and reliable operation, as well as manage potential thermal challenges associated with increased circuit density.
The near-field SW model involves complex mathematical expressions, particularly challenging for applications with multiple or complex sources. Figure 1 illustrates five different scenarios of electromagnetic wave propagation. In the multi-input single-output (MISO) scenario or when the source point is at the sensing point, far-field plane waves cause the antenna to exhibit uniform directional transmission with linear phase differences. On the other hand, near-field spherical waves determined by distance and angle produce nonlinear phase differences between antennas. In the MIMO scenario or when sensing large aperture targets, near-field spherical waves exhibit more complex characteristics compared to far-field plane waves. At long distances, two arrays approximate as point sources forming plane waves. At medium distances, the array acts as a mirror, forming a mirror MISO configuration. At short distances, each antenna acts as a separate point source, leading to complete SW transmission.
1) SW parameter estimation: Classical estimation algorithms such as subspace methods and compression-sensing face challenges from XL arrays. Due to feature value decomposition, some subspace methods require dense computations, while compression-sensing encounters difficulties in sparse recovery due to the additional DoF caused by the SW model. Moreover, compared to far-field tracking, near-field parameter tracking becomes more challenging because it involves rapid deviations with changes in the transmitter-source distance.
2) MIMO-LoS channel: The MIMO-LoS channel has been extensively studied, focusing on maximizing the capacity of the spherical wave channel through optimization of array aperture and antenna position. On the other hand, the unique properties of the MIMO-LoS channel pose challenges to channel estimation, especially when the communication distance is short. In this case, as shown in Figure 1, the channel cannot be divided into the outer product of two array response vectors. When attempting to jointly estimate both the LoS and NLoS channel paths, this aspect presents significant challenges.
3) Array synthesis: Array synthesis is an important link in array signal processing, aiming to optimize antenna excitation coefficients to meet requirements such as beam directionality and bandwidth. It can also optimize the position and number of antennas to achieve the desired beam pattern. Traditional synthesis methods mainly focus on far-field beam patterns, which are simpler than near-field ones. However, due to the nonlinearity in the array response, far-field beam patterns become more complex.
4) Array calibration: The precise beam pointing in array antennas requires precise control of phase and amplitude. Since electronic components need regular calibration due to temperature and time changes. Array calibration involves tasks such as analyzing radiation patterns, calibrating beamforming, and evaluating SNR. However, individually measuring each antenna may be impractical due to the spatial constraints of having a large number of antennas. Moreover, ensuring appropriate element excitation, phase synchronization, and mutual coupling compensation in XL arrays may be time-consuming and prone to errors.
Apart from the above challenges, the significant increase in spatial dimensions will lead to extremely high computational complexity for XL systems. This requires expensive computing resources and hinders real-time applications.
C. Basic Potential
Despite the challenges with XL arrays, there are still some potential areas worth exploring. Besides beam forming gain, XL arrays also bring three basic potentials for S&C.
1) Multi-channel multiplexing: In mmWave MIMO-LoS communication, SW channels have shown high channel rank, enhancing the capability for multiple data streams transmission simultaneously and indirectly improving capacity.
2) Spatial resolution: XL arrays demonstrate strong beamforming capabilities, providing a highly directional beam that not only covers angles but also extends into range, thus achieving high-resolution parameter estimation.
3) Range sensing: Typically, range perception methods use arrival time, which is used for delay estimation in distance calculation, limited by frequency resources for spatial resolution. However, using SW models allows near-field range estimation in narrow-band systems. Combining time of arrival and SW propagation shows strong potential for range estimation.

A. What is SA?
Generally speaking, SA can be defined in two ways:
· As a carefully designed antenna layout with a small number of elements, which achieves specific goals strategically, such as maximizing DoF or minimizing redundancy.
· As a dynamic array, minimizes the number of active antennas while optimizing antenna position/weight, maintaining system performance. This is also known as sparse array synthesis [8].
1. The two definitions reflect different methods of utilizing SA advantages, each suitable for different scenarios and optimization objectives. Here are some typical SAs:
1) Coprime Array (CA): CA is one of the first sparse arrays, introducing closed-form expressions for antenna positions. This means that once the number of elements is given, the antenna positions or SA configuration can be immediately obtained without any exhaustive search mechanism. A N-element CA consists of an array of Q-elements with element spacing Pd and a 2P-1-element array with element spacing Qd, where P and Q are coprime integers such that P < Q, d represents the half wavelength element spacing.
2) Nested Arrays (NAs): NA provides another closed-form expression for antenna positioning, offering a non-redundant array improvement over CA. An N-element two-level NA (assuming N is even) consists of two N-element arrays with spacings d and (N2 + 1)d. Notably, the second array starts from Nd2.
3) Non-Redundant Arrays (NRAs): The design of NRAs has always been a very interesting topic as they can provide the highest number of DoF. Due to the diversity of NRAs, this paper only considers one [9].

C. How to integrate SA into the XL array?
Due to the significant increase in array aperture caused by the addition of antennas, introducing the SA into mmWave/THz XLMIMO systems poses challenges. For example, consider the 128-element two-stage NA described in Section III-A2. This would result in an aperture of 4120 d for practical deployment. To leverage the advantages of SA while considering the number of antennas required for high-frequency communication to overcome path loss challenges, we aim to design more efficient arrays for XL arrays.
Subarray arrays, also known as partially connected hybrid beamforming structures, show promise for practical mmWave/THz systems due to their reduction in hardware complexity by directly connecting each RF chain to the array antennas. Based on this concept, [10] proposed the Wide Separation Multi-Subarray (WSMS) structure, which increases the inter-subarray spacing to take advantage of the multiplexing benefits of the SW model.
Considering that equal subarray spacing in WSMS leads to spatial redundancy, we combine the properties of SA to propose the concept of sparse subarrays. Additionally, we explore typical SAs, producing multi-subarrays (CMS), nested multi-subarrays (NMS), and non-redundant multi-subarrays (NRMS). These sparse subarrays can be designed by treating each subarray as an antenna and adhering to SA design rules.

How will D.XL-SA function?
After studying the advantages of SA and the design considerations for XL-SA, we now delve into the potential applications of XL-SA.
1) Dynamic Near-Field Mixed Beamforming: With the flexibility provided by XL-SA, near-field mixed beamforming becomes more versatile. Figure 3 illustrates four scenarios of hybrid beamforming optimized using SA. For partially connected structures, optimizing the position of subarrays becomes crucial to maximize the near-field effect within a certain number of subarrays. Reducing the number of subarrays to improve energy efficiency while maintaining a reasonable system performance is also important. For fully connected structures, these scenarios can be adjusted from those of partially connected structures, with the difference being the optimization of antenna elements rather than subarrays.
2) Active XL-RIS: Active RISs have a significant advantage in channel capacity compared to passive RISs. Additionally, active RISs facilitate the adoption of more effective channel estimation methods, enabling accurate and timely channel condition estimation [11]. This can potentially solve the issue of significant pilot overhead required for near-field XL-RIS channel estimation [12]. However, high hardware costs and energy consumption limit the practicality of full-active RISs in XL systems. To address this, an active XL-RIS is proposed, where a small number of components are active, while the rest remain passive. Two approaches are considered: pre-designed SA modes (NMS, CMS, and NRMS) and dynamic deployment using an electronic switch to adapt to different scenarios.
3) Enhanced SDMA: Compared to XL-DUA, XL-SA shows greater potential for near-field SDMA due to increased degrees of freedom and spatial resolution. When multiple users close to each other access the system, XL-DUA may face limitations, leading to performance errors. Moreover, its distance resolution decreases as the angle between the user and the array normal increases [13]. To overcome this challenge, XL-SA with a flexible array layout optimizes antenna positioning, enhancing system performance.
4) Enhanced Near-Field ISAC: As mentioned earlier, near-field ISAC shows tremendous potential. Future ISAC systems will require high DoF and high resolution sparse arrays similar to existing sensor systems to achieve advanced sensing applications. Additionally, this will further enhance near-field communication capabilities, making S&C integration in the near-field area more promising. Current far-field or near-field ISACs have limited DoF, which can be enhanced by optimizing the SA of the array layout to from hardware to algorithm improve ISAC.
5) Near-Field Reconfigurable MIMO (PR-MIMO): PR-MIMO was developed to overcome the performance limitations of MIMO systems with limited aperture. Unlike traditional MIMO systems with fixed antenna pattern, PR-MIMO supports dynamic directional adjustment for each antenna, achieving high directivity and adaptive beamforming. However, when using XL arrays, PR-MIMO faces challenges: high hardware costs due to complex circuitry and feeding, as well as increased complexity in directional optimization. Therefore, XL-SA is more suitable for PR-MIMO in XL array configurations. They offer enhanced performance while reducing hardware costs and mode optimization complexity, making them a more appropriate choice for implementing PR-MIMO in XL systems.
6) Enhanced MIMO-LoS Communications: MIMO-LoS plays a crucial role in high-frequency communication, maritime communication, and drone communication. When using XL arrays, SW channel enhancement of MIMO-LoS improves multiplexing and capacity. XL-SA, with its flexible and larger array aperture, outperforms XL-DUA even with the same number of antennas, especially in MIMO-LoS communications. Moreover, optimizing antenna positions can further achieve optimal MIMO-LoS transmission.

To evaluate the potential of XL-SA in near-field S&C applications, we conducted two simulations comparing their performance to that of XL-DUA. Considering actual aperture configurations, without including NA, CA, and NRA as baselines. Simulations were executed at 100 GHz system frequency, 512 antennas, and a 0 dB receive signal-to-noise ratio. A multi-subarray structure consisting of 8 subarrays each containing 64 antennas was considered.
Figure 4 shows the relationship between root range CRB performance and range r, where it is evident that our proposed XL-SA outperforms XL-DUA in terms of root range CRB. Among these arrays, XL-NRMS demonstrates the best performance, highlighting its capability for high spatial resolution. Moreover, the root distance CRB increases with distance, indicating that distance estimation can be more precise in the near field. Alternatively, when the sensor is located at a far-field object such as 120 meters away, due to high estimation errors, XL-DUA is limited. In this context, XL-SA offers an advantage by providing enhanced robustness in distance sensing in such situations.
B. Communication Performance
In this evaluation, we analyzed channel rank as a metric for communication performance. For MIMO scenarios, we considered equal number of transmitters and receivers with a fixed distance of 100 meters between them. We used singular value decomposition to handle the SW channels. We defined effective channel paths as those with singular values greater than 10−3, and then determined the channel rank by counting such singular values. As shown in Figure 5, XL-DUA has a maximum channel rank of 6, allowing only six simultaneous data streams. In contrast, our proposed XL-SA provides more DoF for spatial multiplexing, with XL-NRMS demonstrating the strongest multiplexing capability.
Unresolved Challenges and Directions
To support applications introduced in Section III-D and fully leverage the potential of XL-SA, several challenges need to be addressed seriously.
A. SA
1) SA Design: This paper introduces promising XL-SA using multi-subarray structures, effectively enhancing their S&C performance. Successful implementation paves the way for integrating more effective SA into XL systems, inspiring future research in this field to improve S&C performance across various applications.
2)SA Optimization: Developing real-time algorithms to dynamically optimize the antenna position and number in XL-SA is a significant challenge. The exploration of joint optimization frameworks for XL-SA and other technologies (including near-field hybrid beamforming) has great potential. It provides solutions for various needs, such as maximizing energy efficiency and meeting various requirements in wireless communication systems.
B.S&C
1) Broadband Transmission: In ultra-wideband XL systems, beam steering and near-field effects pose challenges to beam management, channel estimation, and hybrid beamforming. XL-SA offers the potential to reduce computational complexity with fewer antennas. However, algorithm design must leverage SA properties to address spatial challenges and maximize the advantages of XL-SA across different applications.
2) Channel Estimation: Channel estimation for XL-SA presents various challenges in various scenarios. Understanding the SW beam steering effect in broadband systems is crucial for accurate beam space channel reconstruction [14], [15], but XL-SA may exhibit different beam steering trajectories compared to XL-DUAs. In MIMO scenarios, an effective joint NLoS and LoS recovery method should be studied for XL-SA, considering the significant differences between array manifold LoS and NLoS. In XL-RIS applications, active XL-RIS with XL-SA can greatly assist channel estimation by simultaneously receiving and reflecting.
3) Sensing and Tracking: Extensive research has been conducted on near-field sensing using DUA. The introduction of XL-SA opens up opportunities for enhancing sensing applications, transitioning from far-field SA sensing to near-field SA sensing. Moreover, emphasizing the importance of near-field parameter tracking is crucial, despite its limited attention. Finely designed tracking methods for XL-SA in the additional distance parameters within the near-field are necessary to ensure optimal utilization in near-field parameter tracking applications.
4) Dynamic S&C and integration: The ultimate goal of XL-SA is to achieve diverse S&C objectives: improving energy efficiency, expanding near-field coverage, and optimizing trade-offs. Firstly, an efficient SA optimization algorithm enables XL-SA to reduce power consumption while maintaining system performance. Compared to XL-DUA, XLSA demonstrates significant enhancements in SW characteristics when given a fixed number of antennas. Moreover, dynamic antenna positioning provides more DoF for ISAC to optimize system-level S&C trade-offs.
Achieving these goals involves the development of new algorithms and efficient system designs, offering new opportunities for future research in XL-SA.

To this end, we start by exploring near-field XL arrays using SW models, covering their hardware/algorithm challenges, basic potential, and promising applications. The research results indicate that near-field S&C will drive new trends in 6 G technology, while also highlighting the opportunities for further development in this field.
